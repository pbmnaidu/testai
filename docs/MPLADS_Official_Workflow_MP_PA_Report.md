# Official MPLADS Workflow: From MP Recommendation to Execution, Inspection and Closure

## Scope and governing sources

This report describes the operational lifecycle of a work under the Members of Parliament Local Area Development Scheme (MPLADS), starting with the MP office identifying and recommending a work and ending with execution, inspection, public use, handover, records, audit and closure.

The primary rulebook is the **MPLADS Guidelines effective from 1 April 2023**, as administered by the Ministry of Statistics and Programme Implementation (MoSPI). The workflow is also read with later official parliamentary and PIB material describing the eSAKSHI portal, revised fund flow, implementation timelines and subsequent portal changes. State/UT administrative, technical, financial, procurement, building and sectoral-clearance rules continue to apply.

Where a later circular or official clarification changes a value or process, the latest official portal instruction and MoSPI clarification should prevail. The Guidelines themselves state that interpretation of the Guidelines is to be referred to MoSPI and that MoSPI's decision is final.

## 1. The short answer: who is responsible for what?

The most important control point is the separation of roles:

| Actor | Official responsibility | What the actor does not do |
|---|---|---|
| Member of Parliament | Recommends eligible developmental works based on locally felt needs | Does not technically sanction, tender, execute, certify expenditure or act as the district implementing authority |
| MP's PA/office | Receives local proposals, prepares a compliant recommendation file, obtains the MP's decision/signature, submits and tracks it | Cannot substitute for the MP's signature or make a recommendation in the MP's name |
| Nodal District Authority (NDA) | Coordinates the MP's allocation through the MP's chosen nodal district | Is not automatically the sanctioning authority for a work located in another district |
| Implementing District Authority (IDA) | Scrutinizes, sanctions or rejects, selects the Implementing Agency, supervises implementation and reports progress | Cannot approve an ineligible work merely because the MP has recommended it |
| Implementing Agency (IA) | Performs technical scrutiny, estimates, procurement/tendering and physical execution; visits 100% of works | Cannot change scope, site or specification informally |
| User Agency | Receives the completed asset and bears operation, upkeep and maintenance from its own resources | Cannot restrict public access or use the asset for a different purpose without due authority |
| State Nodal Authority | Coordinates and monitors the State/UT programme, conducts inspections and third-party physical audit, arranges audit and training | Does not replace the district's day-to-day execution responsibility |
| MoSPI / Central Nodal Agency | Authorizes and monitors the national programme, operates the central fund-flow architecture and reviews performance | Does not make the MP's local recommendation or execute the district work |

MoSPI's own programme-implementation description states the principle plainly: works are recommended by MPs, while sanction, execution and monitoring are undertaken by District Authorities under the applicable State/UT rules. [1]

## 2. The complete lifecycle in one view

```text
Local need / proposal
        ↓
MP office intake and eligibility screening
        ↓
MP decides priority and signs the prescribed recommendation
        ↓
Recommendation submitted through eSAKSHI / prescribed Annexure-II format
        ↓
Receipt timestamp and work ID created
        ↓
Implementing District Authority scrutiny
        ├── rejection with reasons within the prescribed period
        └── technical, financial and administrative sanction
                    ↓
        User Agency maintenance undertaking + statutory clearances
                    ↓
        Implementing Agency selected under State/UT rules
                    ↓
        Technical estimate, tender/procurement and work order
                    ↓
        Execution, site visits, progress records and eSAKSHI uploads
                    ↓
        Just-in-time payment against actual execution / vendor bills
                    ↓
        Completion documentation, photographs, measurement and bill checks
                    ↓
        IA inspection + District monitoring + State/third-party sampling
                    ↓
        Asset handover, public use, plaque and asset register
                    ↓
        Utilization / audit / public dashboard / closure
```

The official eSAKSHI description says that recommendation, District Authority sanction and Implementing Agency execution are integrated on one platform, with real-time status, progress reports and dashboards. The revised fund-flow system is intended to make payment directly to vendors on a just-in-time basis, without parking scheme funds in physical district accounts. [2]

## 3. Stage 0 — Annual setup by the MP and PA

### 3.1 Select the nodal district

At the beginning of the MP's term, the MP gives a choice of Nodal District to the Central Nodal Agency in the prescribed format. The normal geography is:

- An elected Lok Sabha MP: works in the constituency; if the constituency spans multiple districts, the MP selects one nodal district and may recommend in the other constituent districts as permitted.
- An elected Rajya Sabha MP: works in one or more districts in the State of election, subject to the Guidelines.
- A nominated MP: works anywhere in the country, subject to the Guidelines.

The district where the work is located is the relevant **Implementing District**. It may be the same as the Nodal District or a different district. If it is different, the MP's PA should copy the recommendation to the Nodal District Authority and the District Authority where the work will actually be executed, and track both offices.

### 3.2 Know the authorization and planning limits

The annual entitlement is ₹5 crore per MP. From 1 April 2023, the annual amount is authorized in a single instalment at the beginning of the financial year or at the start of the MP's term under the revised fund-flow procedure. Unspent balances are carried forward under the scheme's rules. [3]

The MP office should maintain, at minimum:

1. An annual entitlement and balance register.
2. A recommended-work register.
3. A sanctioned-work register.
4. An implementation and payment tracker.
5. A completed-asset and inspection register.
6. A rejection, resubmission and exception register.

The 2023 Guidelines advise MPs to recommend, each year, works costing at least 15% of total entitlement in areas predominantly inhabited by Scheduled Castes and at least 7.5% in areas predominantly inhabited by Scheduled Tribes. The PA should therefore add an SC/ST classification field at the idea stage rather than trying to calculate it at the end of the year. [4]

### 3.3 Build a constituency “shelf of needs,” not a list of promised sanctions

The IDA is required to maintain and make available a suggestive “Shelf of Projects.” It is only suggestive; the MP retains flexibility to go beyond it if the proposed work complies with the Guidelines. The PA should use this shelf as a technical starting point, not as a substitute for a site-specific scrutiny.

Good MP-office planning questions are:

- Is the need public and locally evidenced?
- Is there a clearly identified public site and User Agency?
- Is the asset likely to be used immediately after completion?
- Is there a realistic estimate and a complete funding source?
- Is the work a new durable asset, or is it actually routine maintenance, a private benefit or an ineligible reimbursement?
- Can the User Agency maintain it after handover?
- Does the proposal duplicate another sanctioned or ongoing work?
- Will the work help the annual SC/ST planning benchmark where applicable?

The PA should record the answers in a one-page concept note before asking the MP to approve the recommendation.

## 4. Stage 1 — Proposal intake and MP-office pre-screening

### 4.1 Where proposals may come from

Residents, elected local bodies, schools, hospitals, line departments, community organizations and other stakeholders may bring needs to the MP office. They are sources of information and requests. They are not the legal recommender under MPLADS.

The official recommendation must come from the MP in the prescribed form. The PA may prepare the draft, coordinate technical inputs and upload data, but the MP must make the decision and authorize the recommendation. The PA should never sign or transmit a “recommendation of works” as if it were the MP.

### 4.2 Minimum intake record

For each proposed work, the PA should create a unique internal proposal number and capture:

| Field | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| MP name, House and term | Establishes the recommending authority and entitlement ledger |
| Proposed work title | Must be specific enough to identify the deliverable |
| Sector and work category | Supports eligibility and reporting |
| Exact location | Enables district routing, land and site verification |
| Village/ward/block/ULB/tehsil/district | Prevents geographic ambiguity |
| Approximate cost | Must be realistic and tied to the proposed scope |
| Proposed User Agency | Needed for operation, upkeep and maintenance undertaking |
| Land ownership/status | Immovable assets generally require Government-owned land or an approved arrangement under the Guidelines |
| Public-access statement | Ensures the asset is for public good without discriminatory restriction |
| Source of estimate | Line department, engineer, schedule of rates or DPR reference |
| Convergence details | Prevents duplicate funding and identifies which source funds first |
| SC/ST area flag | Supports the annual planning benchmark |
| Photographs/site note | Helps detect wrong site, existing asset or private premises before recommendation |
| Statutory clearance risk | Identifies road, forest, water, railway, electricity, land, heritage and building approvals |
| Conflict/duplication check | Avoids financing a work already sanctioned or completed under another scheme |

### 4.3 Eligibility gate before the MP signs

The central test is whether the work creates a durable public asset for public good, with no restriction on access and usage to a section of society. The 2023 Guidelines permit immovable public assets on Government-owned land and movable public assets for Government-owned or Government-controlled institutions, including Government-aided institutions after the relevant 2023 clarification. [5]

Common permissible sectors include public/community buildings, public conveniences, safety and security, education, public health, drinking water and sanitation, irrigation, drainage and flood control, animal husbandry, agriculture and farmer welfare, energy, roads and pathways, environment, sports, parks and other works consistent with the objectives and provisions of the Guidelines.

The PA should reject or redesign a proposal before it reaches the MP if it is primarily:

- operation or maintenance expenditure;
- construction of a residential building;
- a commercial or private-establishment work;
- a grant, loan, cash benefit or contribution to a relief fund;
- acquisition of land or compensation for land;
- reimbursement for an already completed or partly completed work;
- a work for individual/family benefit, except permitted assistive devices for identified differently abled beneficiaries;
- pooling with CSR funds;
- a religious work, a work within a place of religious worship or on land belonging to a religious faith/group;
- a Welcome Gate/Swagat Dwar;
- a work in an unauthorized colony; or
- recurring expenditure.

Repair and renovation of immovable assets can be considered within the applicable ceiling and conditions. A later official parliamentary reply records the current ceiling as up to 10% of the MP's total authorization in a financial year for all such repairs and renovations together, and an earlier 2023 PDF text should not be read in isolation from later official modifications. [6]

### 4.4 Convergence screen

MPLADS can be pooled with an individual or stand-alone project of another Central or State Government scheme or a local body if the work is otherwise eligible under MPLADS and duplication is avoided. Where convergence is used, the other scheme's funds should be used first and MPLADS should bridge the remaining gap needed to complete the eligible project. The MP can recommend such a work through eSAKSHI. [7]

The PA file should contain:

1. the other scheme's sanction or approved work reference;
2. the total project cost;
3. the amount already committed from the other scheme/local body;
4. the amount requested from MPLADS;
5. a written no-duplication statement; and
6. the proposed funding order.

## 5. Stage 2 — The MP makes the official recommendation

### 5.1 Prescribed recommendation

The 2023 Guidelines provide an Annexure-II format for recommending works. The format includes the MP's name, House and term, the District Authority addressed, the nature of each work, location and approximate cost. The recommendation should be on the MP's letterhead and signed by the MP. The official portal now carries the recommendation workflow through eSAKSHI, but the PA should preserve the signed source document in the office file.

The MP's letter should say, in substance:

> I recommend that the following works may please be scrutinized and sanctioned from MPLADS funds. The technical, financial and administrative sanction may be issued after due scrutiny. The sanctioned works should be undertaken and completed as per the MPLADS Guidelines. I may please be kept informed of sanction and progress.

The PA should not promise sanction, contractor selection, completion date or payment in the letter. Those are District Authority and Implementing Agency functions.

### 5.2 Submission protocol

For each submission, the PA should:

1. Obtain the MP's final approval and signature/portal authentication.
2. Submit the work through the eSAKSHI workflow and attach the signed recommendation and supporting documents required by the portal.
3. If the work is in a district outside the MP's nodal district, send the prescribed copy to the relevant District Authority as well as the NDA.
4. Record the exact date and time of receipt/portal submission.
5. Save the portal acknowledgement, work ID and initial status.
6. Start a 45-day sanction/rejection calendar from the date the recommendation reaches the IDA.

The receipt date is a critical control. “Sent by the PA” and “received by the IDA” are not necessarily the same date. The PA should track the latter and retain proof.

### 5.3 What the MP can change and when

Before sanction, the MP can change or cancel the recommended work or site. The IDA may change the work or site only with the MP's concurrence. Once the work has been sanctioned, the normal rule is that no change should be made. A successor MP cannot change a work recommended by the predecessor, even if the successor is re-elected from the same constituency.

If a sanctioned work later becomes impossible, the matter must be treated as an exception and routed through the IDA, State Nodal Authority and the eSAKSHI/Central Nodal Agency process with written reasons. A 2025 official parliamentary reply records that eSAKSHI has a pull-back/status-change workflow for valid reasons after sanction, subject to due recording and approval; that should not be used as a routine method for changing an MP's sanctioned promise. [8]

## 6. Stage 3 — District scrutiny and sanction/rejection

### 6.1 The 45-day rule

The Implementing District Authority must issue the sanction or rejection in respect of the MP's recommendation within 45 days from the date the recommendation is received by the IDA. If the proposal cannot be executed, the reasons should be communicated to the MP and the relevant authorities as early as possible.

The Ministry's official 2025 explanation also identifies the following implementation controls:

- sanction/rejection within 45 days;
- the sanction letter should generally allow no more than one year for completion by the Implementing Agency;
- the Ministry monitors works pending sanction beyond 45 days, works not completed after one year of sanction and works with no payment three months after sanction. [9]

The period during which a Model Code of Conduct applies to the relevant IDA jurisdiction is not counted in the relevant time limits under the Guidelines.

### 6.2 IDA scrutiny checklist

Before sanctioning, the IDA should establish all of the following:

1. **Geographic eligibility:** the work is within the permissible region for the MP, or falls under an applicable outside-region or calamity provision.
2. **Asset eligibility:** the work is a permissible durable public asset and is not on the prohibited list.
3. **Land/site:** the site is available, legally usable and suitable; land ownership and encumbrance issues are resolved.
4. **Public benefit:** access and use are not restricted to a private, commercial or religious group.
5. **No duplication:** the work is not already completed, sanctioned or fully funded under another programme.
6. **Technical feasibility:** the line department/engineer can technically execute the work.
7. **Estimate:** the cost is prepared under the State/UT schedule of rates or other applicable technical procedure.
8. **Full funding:** if the estimate exceeds the amount initially indicated by the MP, the MP has consented to and allocated the full estimated cost; otherwise the work should not be sanctioned as an underfunded incomplete project.
9. **User Agency:** a written undertaking has been obtained that the User Agency will bear operation, upkeep and maintenance from its own resources.
10. **Statutory/regulatory clearances:** all necessary clearances are obtained from competent authorities before sanction.
11. **Minimum work value:** normally at least ₹2.5 lakh per individual work; a lower amount requires reasons to be recorded in the sanction letter.
12. **Annual entitlement:** the total estimated amount of all sanctioned works does not exceed the MP's entitlement up to that financial year, including applicable interest and redistribution.
13. **Implementing Agency:** a capable agency is selected under State/UT rules; domain-specific Central Government organizations are selected where the work belongs to their domain.

### 6.3 The sanction order

The sanction order should be treated as the work's control document. It should contain, at least:

- unique work ID and MP details;
- exact approved work description and location;
- approved estimate and funding sources;
- land/site and User Agency details;
- Implementing Agency;
- technical, administrative and financial sanction references;
- statutory-clearance references;
- procurement/tendering conditions;
- completion deadline, normally not exceeding one year;
- special justification if more than one year is necessary;
- reporting, inspection, photograph and eSAKSHI-upload requirements;
- payment conditions linked to actual progress/vendor bills;
- consequences under State rules for failure or delay; and
- copy to the MP.

The PA should obtain and file the sanction order even if the portal shows “sanctioned.” The portal status and the signed sanction order should agree.

## 7. Stage 4 — Selection of the Implementing Agency and execution

### 7.1 Implementing Agency selection

The IDA selects the appropriate Implementing Agency. Selection must follow the applicable State/UT rules. Depending on the work, the IA may be a Government department, local government, Central Government organization, Gram Panchayat/ULB or another eligible agency under the Guidelines.

The MP may recommend the need and location, but the MP/PA should not nominate a preferred contractor or direct procurement. The IDA/IA must use the State's established scrutiny, estimation, tendering and administrative procedure.

### 7.2 Technical and procurement work

The IA must:

1. confirm technical feasibility;
2. prepare a detailed estimate under the applicable schedule of rates;
3. obtain technical sanction from the competent authority;
4. follow open and transparent bidding/procurement where required;
5. issue the work order and record the vendor/contractor;
6. establish the baseline scope, quantities, specifications and completion date; and
7. upload the required sanction, estimate, work-order and related documents into eSAKSHI.

The Implementing Agency is responsible for timely implementation. It must maintain a work register showing physical and financial progress and site visits, and it must inspect 100% of the works.

### 7.3 Fund flow and payments

Under the revised eSAKSHI fund-flow model, funds are intended to be released just in time and paid directly to the vendor on the basis of an electronically authorized demand/actual execution rather than being parked in physical district-level accounts. The official Parliamentary material describes virtual drawing limits, PFMS mapping and direct vendor payment as part of the revised architecture. [10]

For the MP office, this means:

- do not treat “sanctioned” as “money paid”;
- do not treat “money authorized” as “work completed”;
- track sanctioned amount, vendor demand, payment amount, balance and physical progress separately;
- ask for the payment reference and linked work stage where a work is reported as paid; and
- flag works with no payment three months after sanction for district review.

## 8. Stage 5 — Monitoring while the work is underway

### 8.1 Implementing Agency: 100% inspection

Officers of the IA must regularly visit the work site and check that work is progressing according to the approved procedure, specifications and time schedule. The IA must inspect 100% of the works and record site visits in the work register.

The IA's site note should identify:

- date, time and officer;
- GPS/location or clearly identifiable site reference where available;
- work stage and quantities executed;
- material/specification conformity;
- labour/vendor/contractor status;
- expenditure and bill stage;
- photographs from consistent viewpoints;
- deviations, defects or risks;
- corrective action and responsible person; and
- next milestone and expected date.

### 8.2 District Authority: at least 10% of works under implementation

The District Authority is responsible for overall monitoring and supervision at district level. It must inspect at least 10% of the works under implementation every year and should involve the MP concerned to the extent feasible. It must also review MPLADS implementation with the Implementing Agencies at least once every month, inviting the MP.

The district work register should be maintained for each MP and each tenure and should contain the position of every recommended work, including photographs when completed. All works executed for trusts and societies must be inspected, and inspection registers must be maintained for such works.

### 8.3 State Nodal Authority: inspection and third-party physical audit

State Nodal Department officers not below the rank prescribed by the Guidelines should make regular field visits and inspect at least 1% of MPLADS works by value in every district each year, with a State-level inspection register and follow-up action.

In addition, each State/UT must conduct annual third-party inspection, including physical audit and quality check, in each district using these criteria:

- all works costing ₹25 lakh or more: compulsory coverage;
- 50% of works costing between ₹15 lakh and ₹25 lakh: coverage;
- the remaining works: a sample of at least 50, balanced across cost, SC/ST areas, MP-wise work and works of societies, trusts, cooperative societies and Bar Associations.

Copies of these inspection reports are to be submitted to the Central Nodal Agency annually by 30 September of the next financial year. [11]

## 9. Stage 6 — Completion, final verification and public use

### 9.1 What “completed” should mean in the file

The status should not be changed to “completed” merely because construction appears substantially finished. The completion packet should establish:

1. the approved scope is fully delivered;
2. measurements and quantities are recorded;
3. the final technical check is complete;
4. the final bill is verified;
5. actual expenditure is reconciled with the sanction;
6. savings, if any, are identified and returned/adjusted under the applicable process;
7. the User Agency has accepted the asset;
8. the asset is available for its intended public use;
9. the required plaque is installed;
10. photographs and documents are uploaded;
11. the asset is entered in the Asset Register; and
12. the work is included in the public list/dashboard.

The official Guidelines state that, as soon as a work is completed, it should be put to public use. A permanent stone/metal plaque should identify the work as an MPLADS work and show the cost, commencement date, completion date, inauguration date and name of the recommending MP. The Guidelines also require lists of completed and ongoing works to be displayed by the relevant public authorities and posted on websites wherever applicable. [12]

### 9.2 Completion documents

The PA should ask the District Authority/IA for the following completion evidence, subject to the State's prescribed format:

- work completion certificate;
- final measurement book or measurement abstract;
- final technical inspection note;
- final bill and vendor payment reference;
- before/during/after photographs;
- installed plaque photograph showing the details clearly;
- completion date and inauguration date, if applicable;
- User Agency acceptance/handover certificate;
- User Agency maintenance undertaking/record;
- savings/refund statement, if applicable;
- Asset Register entry;
- updated eSAKSHI work status; and
- public dashboard/listing confirmation.

### 9.3 Who inspects after completion?

The official system does not reduce post-completion control to one ceremonial visit by one officer. It uses several overlapping controls:

- **Implementing Agency:** inspection of all works and completion documentation;
- **District Authority:** overall supervision, at least 10% annual inspection of works under implementation, monthly review, and inspection of all trust/society works;
- **State Nodal Authority:** at least 1% annual inspection by value in each district and separate annual third-party physical audit/quality check using the cost-based sampling rules;
- **MoSPI/Central Nodal Agency:** national review, dashboard scrutiny and third-party monitoring as arranged by the Ministry;
- **User Agency:** custody, operation, upkeep and maintenance after handover; and
- **Public:** access to the asset and visibility of the project details through the plaque, public lists and portal information.

For an MP/PA, the practical rule is: request an official completion packet and a documented inspection/verification note, not merely an inauguration photograph.

## 10. Stage 7 — Handover, maintenance and asset records

The User Agency is the custodian of the asset and is responsible for operation, upkeep and maintenance at its own cost. The IDA must obtain the User Agency's written willingness to bear those costs before sanction. The User Agency should use the asset for the sanctioned public purpose and preserve public access.

The District Authority should maintain an Asset Register for assets created with MPLADS funds and transferred to User Agencies or beneficiaries, and post the data in the public domain. The PA should keep a copy of the handover certificate and the asset's long-term custodian in the MP office's completed-work register.

For movable assets, the relevant Government institution should record them in its stock register. The asset should carry the prescribed MPLADS marking as far as possible.

## 11. Stage 8 — Utilization certificates, audit and closure

The fund-flow procedure operates with PFMS and eSAKSHI records, and the portal provides for work and financial monitoring. The Guidelines also require audit arrangements at State and District levels. The State and District authorities must arrange audit through auditors from the panel approved by the Accountant General of the State/UT, and audit certificates for the preceding year's MPLADS funds are to be submitted to the Central Nodal Agency by 30 September each year, with comments and follow-up on objections.

For the MP/PA office, closure should mean that the work is not only marked “completed” but also financially and administratively reconciled:

| Closure control | Evidence to keep |
|---|---|
| Scope completed | Completion certificate and final technical note |
| Cost reconciled | Final bill, expenditure statement, savings/refund record |
| Payment completed | Vendor payment reference and eSAKSHI/PFMS status |
| Asset accepted | Handover certificate and User Agency acknowledgement |
| Public use enabled | Public-use note, operating location and access details |
| Inspection trail | IA inspection, DA/State inspection where selected, corrective-action closure |
| Visibility | Plaque photograph and public dashboard/listing |
| Custody and maintenance | User Agency, undertaking and Asset Register entry |
| Audit trail | UC, audit certificate or reference and objection status |

## 12. MP and PA office SOP — practical operating procedure

### A. Before the MP approves a proposal

- Create an internal proposal ID.
- Verify district, constituency and permissible geography.
- Confirm public land/site or lawful Government-controlled location.
- Confirm proposed User Agency and obtain a preliminary maintenance commitment.
- Check prohibited-work list and duplication.
- Obtain a technical concept/rough estimate from the competent line department where possible.
- Classify SC/ST area, convergence and sector.
- Confirm the MP's available balance and that the full estimated cost can be funded.
- Identify statutory clearances likely to be required.
- Prepare a one-page recommendation brief for the MP.

### B. At the moment of recommendation

- Use the prescribed Annexure-II fields.
- Put the recommendation on the MP's letterhead.
- Obtain the MP's signature or authorized eSAKSHI authentication.
- Attach the signed recommendation and required supporting records.
- Submit through eSAKSHI.
- Send any prescribed copy to the NDA and the implementing district.
- Save acknowledgement, work ID, receipt date and portal status.

### C. During the 45-day sanction window

- Day 0: record receipt by the IDA.
- Day 7–10: confirm the file is visible to the IDA and no document is missing.
- Day 15–20: request the scrutiny status through the official channel if no movement is visible.
- Day 30: escalate internally to the MP's office head and record the delay.
- Day 45: if there is no sanction/rejection, send a formal status request to the IDA with the work ID and receipt proof, copying the NDA/State Nodal Authority as appropriate.

These are office-control suggestions; the official rule is the 45-day sanction/rejection requirement, not a PA's authority to order the district.

### D. After sanction

- File the sanction order and verify that scope, location, amount, IA and deadline match the MP's recommendation.
- Confirm the User Agency undertaking and statutory clearances.
- Record the IA, technical sanction, tender/work-order date and vendor.
- Track monthly physical progress and payment separately.
- Attend or request the monthly district review where appropriate.
- Ask for site photographs and inspection notes at defined milestones.
- Flag no-payment situations at three months after sanction.
- Flag one-year completion slippage and obtain the written reason/extension basis.

### E. At completion

- Do not close the work from an inauguration photograph alone.
- Obtain the completion packet, final bill, measurements, handover, plaque, asset register and payment confirmation.
- Request the final inspection/verification note.
- Confirm that the asset is in public use and the User Agency has custody.
- Check that the public list/dashboard is updated.
- Archive the full file by MP term and financial year.

## 13. Officer inspection checklist

An inspection officer's report should answer the following questions:

### Identity and location

- Is this the sanctioned work and the sanctioned site?
- Does the work ID, plaque, location and asset description match the records?
- Is the asset located on the approved land/site?

### Physical completion and quality

- What quantities and components were sanctioned?
- What quantities and components are physically present?
- Are materials, dimensions, specifications and workmanship consistent with the technical sanction?
- Are there visible defects, unsafe conditions or unfinished portions?
- Is the asset functional and capable of being put to public use?

### Financial and procurement control

- Is expenditure within the sanctioned amount?
- Are measurements, bills and vendor payments linked to actual execution?
- Are savings/refunds recorded?
- Was the Implementing Agency and vendor selected under applicable rules?

### Public use and custody

- Is the asset open to the public without prohibited discrimination?
- Is the User Agency identified and in possession?
- Is the maintenance undertaking on file?
- Is the asset being used for the sanctioned purpose?

### Transparency and record

- Is the plaque installed and readable?
- Are before/after photographs available?
- Is the Asset Register updated?
- Is the work status updated on eSAKSHI/public dashboard?
- Have defects and corrective actions been assigned and closed?

## 14. Exceptions and red flags

### Rejection

The IDA should communicate reasons for rejection. The PA should not simply resubmit the same work. First classify the reason: ineligible asset, land/site, missing clearance, duplicate funding, insufficient balance, incomplete estimate, no User Agency, or technical infeasibility. Correct only the defect that is legally correctable.

### Cost higher than MP indication

The work may be sanctioned only if the MP consents to and allocates the full estimated cost. The PA should not allow a work to proceed with an unrecorded “balance will be arranged later” assumption.

### Site or scope change

Before sanction, the MP can change/cancel. After sanction, the normal rule prohibits change. Any unavoidable post-sanction exception must be documented and processed through the IDA/State/CNA/eSAKSHI route.

### Work stopped or abandoned

If stoppage or abandonment becomes unavoidable, the matter should go to the State Nodal Authority with full justification, under intimation to the Central Nodal Agency and the MP. The district should preserve responsibility-fixing and recovery/corrective-action records.

### Trusts, societies, cooperatives and Bar Associations

These cases need extra scrutiny of legal status, reputation, non-profit character, land/title, public access, agreement, maintenance and inspection. A later official parliamentary answer records a ceiling of ₹50 lakh in a financial year for all such entities together and ₹1 crore for a single entity during the MP's term, subject to the current Guidelines and official modifications. [13]

### Works outside the normal region

The 2023 PDF contains an older ₹25 lakh ceiling in para 3.1.2.1, but official 2025 parliamentary material records the applicable modified ceiling as ₹50 lakh per financial year for an elected MP's recommendations outside the usual region, except in a calamity. The PA should use the current eSAKSHI validation and latest MoSPI circular rather than hard-coding the older value. [6]

### No maintenance commitment

Do not allow the recommendation to move to sanction without a written User Agency undertaking. An asset with no identified custodian is a high-risk proposal even if the construction itself is eligible.

### “Completed” without use

An asset is not operationally complete merely because civil work has stopped. If it is not functional, handed over, marked, entered in the Asset Register and available for public use, the PA should treat it as a closure exception.

```text
DRAFT
 → MP_APPROVAL_PENDING
 → RECOMMENDED
 → RECEIVED_BY_IDA
 → SCRUTINY
 → SANCTIONED or REJECTED
 → IA_SELECTED
 → TECHNICAL_SANCTIONED
 → TENDER/WORK_ORDER
 → ONGOING
 → PAYMENT_STAGE_PENDING
 → PAYMENT_RELEASED
 → COMPLETION_SUBMITTED
 → IA_VERIFIED
 → DA_VERIFIED / INSPECTION_SELECTED
 → HANDED_OVER
 → PUBLIC_USE
 → CLOSED
```

Every transition should store:

- actor and role;
- date/time;
- digital authentication or signed document;
- reason/comment;
- supporting documents;
- amount before and after the transition;
- work location;
- inspection findings and corrective actions; and
- immutable audit history.

Important automated controls are:

1. 45-day sanction/rejection alert from IDA receipt.
2. One-year completion alert from sanction.
3. Three-month no-payment alert after sanction.
4. MP entitlement and full-cost validation.
5. Duplicate site/work/asset warning.
6. Missing User Agency undertaking block.
7. Missing statutory-clearance block.
8. Prohibited-sector and prohibited-location warning.
9. SC/ST planning dashboard.
10. Inspection quota dashboard: IA 100%, DA 10%, State 1% by value, and third-party sample.
11. Completion packet checklist block before “completed.”
12. Handover and Asset Register block before “closed.”

## Bottom line for an MP and PA

The MP's job is to identify a public need, choose a legally eligible and fundable work, recommend it personally through the prescribed channel and monitor whether the administration acts within time. The PA's job is to make that recommendation complete, traceable and compliant, then maintain the follow-up record.

The officer's job begins after receipt: scrutinize, obtain clearances and maintenance commitment, sanction or reject within 45 days, select the Implementing Agency, supervise execution, make payment only against actual work, inspect and document the asset, hand it to the User Agency and keep the public record and audit trail complete.

The safest practical rule is:

> **No MP promise without an eligibility file; no sanction without land, clearance, estimate and maintenance commitment; no payment without execution evidence; no “completion” without inspection and handover; no closure without the Asset Register and audit trail.**
